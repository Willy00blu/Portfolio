Recommended apps4

The four apps I use every day: what they are, why I use them and how I set them up.

ObsidianPocketWispr Flow and the shortcutsRaycast
the shelf

Obsidian

Notes on the computer, in text files, linked to each other.

What it is

Obsidian is a note-taking app that saves notes on your own computer, as plain-text Markdown files. Notes link to each other and a graph view shows how they connect; for diagrams there's an endless canvas. It extends with thousands of plugins.

From the official site, obsidian.md ↗
the recorder

Pocket

It records the lecture and turns it into notes.

What it is

Pocket is a recorder the size of a credit card that clips onto the back of the phone. It records lectures and meetings with two microphones, even without the phone nearby; when the app is opened, the recording becomes a transcript, a summary, to-dos and a mind map. Transcription works in more than 120 languages.

From the official site, heypocket.com ↗
dictation

Wispr Flow and the shortcuts

Dictating instead of typing, and a shortcut cleans up the Italian.

What it is

Wispr Flow turns speech into written text in any app: you speak and the text appears where you're typing. It removes filler words, adds punctuation and tidies the form. It works in more than 100 languages, on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android.

From the official site, wisprflow.ai ↗
the shortcuts

Raycast

Opening, searching and pasting without the mouse.

What it is

Raycast is a launcher: a bar that opens from the keyboard and leads to the tools on the computer. Inside there are snippets, texts inserted by typing a keyword, quicklinks, the history of what was copied, window management and a store of extensions for other apps.

From the official site, raycast.com ↗
