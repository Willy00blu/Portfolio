Clinical Risk Detector for Physiotherapy

A tool that, from the recorded visit, suggests to the physiotherapist whether to treat the patient or refer them to a doctor; I coordinate the student team building it.

Role Team Project Manager·ongoing

At PoliMi Data Scientists, the student association for data science and AI at Politecnico di Milano, I coordinate, as project manager, a team of students who are working, together with some physiotherapists, on Clinical Risk Detector for Physiotherapy, a decision-support tool. More and more patients go to a physiotherapist without seeing a doctor first, and the physiotherapist has to work out whether the case is theirs or should be sent elsewhere. The finished tool will have to listen to the visit, answer a list of questions and suggest a course of action, showing why, while the decision stays with the physiotherapist.

Warning signs

In the profession, warning signs are called flags, with different colours for different levels of risk. Red flags indicate high risk: for example, pain that wakes you up at night, fever, weight lost for no reason, or a trauma with signs of a fracture. In private practice, which is the growing sector, the physiotherapist is often alone, whereas in a hospital there is always a doctor nearby.

The decision-support tool

The tool is a clinical decision-support system, for what is known as screening for referral: working out who should be referred to another professional. Besides suggesting a course of action, it flags what doesn't add up between what was said during the visit and the questions that matter.

The project manager role

As project manager I steer the project's direction, organise the weekly meetings, keep in touch with the physiotherapists who collaborate with us and reach out to physiotherapy practices, which are the ones the finished tool will have to help. The team is small and changes, because students have exams, internships and jobs, so part of the work is also finding someone to come in when someone leaves.
