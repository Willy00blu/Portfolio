Axioma

A week of meals, computed as an optimization problem.

Role On my own·finished

Axioma is a program that plans a week of meals and draws up the shopping list. You pick the foods you like and switch on the rules to follow; the program treats the week as an optimisation problem: among the quantities that respect the rules, it looks for the best ones. It lets you shop for a few days knowing you'll eat well, without weighing everything, a need I had as a student living away from home. It's a small project, but it was one of the first in which what I was studying in Computer Engineering became a tool I use. The app is online and the code is on GitHub.

The starting sheet on Notion

The model: variables, constraints and objective

The quantities are chosen by a solver, a program built specifically for optimisation problems. The model is written with PuLP, a Python library, and solved by CBC, the solver that comes with PuLP.

Foods, units and axioms

The name comes from logic, where a theory starts from a few rules taken as true, the axioms, and everything else follows from them. I rethought the diet the way you build a theory: the foods arranged as a tree, from general categories down to single foods; the units, that is the meal, the day and the week; and axioms that say when a meal is valid, how often a food can come back, when the week is balanced. In the app every axiom is a rule you switch on with a tick: how to build the meals, how often meat, meal prep. Each food also has one to five stars, to say how much you like it.

The app and the tools

The interface is built with Streamlit. Behind it sit the model, the rules and the solver; between the two parts there are data contracts, which fix what is asked of the solver and what it answers.
